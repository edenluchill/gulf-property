Founders' & Technology Partnership Agreement

Pinzos — AI-Driven Real Estate Platform (Middle East)

This Founders' & Technology Partnership Agreement (this "Agreement") is made as of the date of the last signature below (the "Effective Date") by and between:

  • Party A — Founding Technology Partner (contributes and owns 100% of the Platform technology): FINDHOMEGO AI TECH INC., a corporation incorporated in British Columbia, Canada, with its registered office in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada (notice details in Schedule 1) ("Findhomego").
  • Party B — Operating Partner: the individuals in Schedule 1 (the "Founders"), jointly and severally, on their own behalf and on behalf of the operating company to be incorporated by the Founders in Dubai, UAE (mainland or DIFC, as the Parties confirm in writing), which shall adopt this Agreement on incorporation (the "Company").

Recitals

(A) Party A independently designed, developed and owns a proprietary AI-driven real estate SaaS platform, including its source code, multi-agent AI architecture, algorithms, data models and infrastructure (the "Platform").
(B) Party B has real estate brokerage expertise, local market access and operational capability in the region.
(C) The Parties agree to run the business together as partners: Party A contributes its independently-owned Platform (making it available for the Company to use) and holds a minority equity stake; Party B contributes local operations, market access and capital; and the Company operates and monetises the Platform, on the terms below.
(D) The Parties enter into this Agreement as long-term partners with a shared interest in the success of the business, and shall act toward each other in good faith. The Company's right to operate is provided for the full Term and is not revocable at will — it may be suspended or ended only for cause as expressly set out in Sections 7.2 and 9.

1. Definitions

  • "Gross Revenue" means all amounts actually received by or on behalf of the Company from operating or monetising the Platform by any means — including SaaS subscriptions, platform access and membership fees, advertising and sponsorship revenue, featured/promoted-listing and placement fees, lead-generation and referral fees, data-licensing and API fees, and per-transaction platform fees — net of (i) refunds/chargebacks paid, (ii) payment-processor fees, and (iii) VAT/sales taxes collected for an authority; measured before any operating cost. It does not include the Company's or any Founder's own real-estate brokerage commissions from acting as broker of record on a property sale or lease.
  • "Territory" means the Middle East (the GCC member states together with the wider Middle East region; the Parties may agree a specific country list in a schedule).
  • "Platform IP" means all Intellectual Property Rights in the Platform (source/object code, AI architectures and prompts, algorithms, data schemas, documentation).
  • "Third-Party Operating Costs" means pass-through third-party costs to run the Platform (cloud hosting, storage/CDN, AI/model APIs, payment-processor fees, data feeds).

2. Technology Ownership & Platform Rights

  • 2.1 Ownership. Party A is and remains the sole owner of 100% of the Platform IP. Nothing here transfers or co-owns any Platform IP. All improvements and derivatives created by or for Party A vest in Party A.
  • 2.2 The Company's Right to Operate the Platform. Party A makes the Platform available for the Company to operate the joint business in the Territory for the Term — a non-transferable, non-sublicensable right to use and operate the Platform (except to end users in the ordinary course). Party A retains full ownership (2.1); this is a right to use, not a transfer. The right is not revocable at will; it may be suspended or ended only for cause as expressly set out in Sections 7.2 and 9.
  • 2.3 The Company's Exclusive Rights in the Territory — Conditioned on Performance. Within the Territory (the Middle East), for as long as the Company meets the milestones in Schedule 2, the Company is the sole and exclusive partner for the Platform — Party A will not bring any other operator or partner into the Territory. If the Company falls below the milestones for two consecutive quarters, then after good-faith discussion the Company's exclusivity narrows to the countries where it is actively operating, and for countries it is not working Party A regains the freedom to develop them (alone or with others). In any country where the Company is active, Party A shall not let a direct competitor of the Company use the Platform. (Party A's use of the Platform in its own markets outside the Territory does not affect the Company's exclusivity here.)
  • 2.4 Restrictions. The Company and Founders shall not, and shall ensure personnel do not: (a) copy, decompile or reverse-engineer the Platform; (b) build a competing platform using knowledge of its confidential architecture; or (c) remove any proprietary notice. Survives termination.
  • 2.5 Background / Pre-Existing IP & Feedback. The Platform is Party A's pre-existing proprietary technology, independently developed, owned and operated by Party A prior to and independently of this Agreement (including for Party A's existing Canadian real-estate website); this Agreement provides a right to use, not a transfer of ownership. The Founders' ideas, suggestions and feedback during the partnership are a welcome and valued part of their contribution as partners: Party A may use them freely, and — consistent with 2.1 — the resulting improvements to the Platform vest in Party A, that contribution being rewarded through the Founders' equity and the Company's growth rather than through ownership of the technology.
  • 2.6 Technology vs. Brand. The (a) Platform / technology IP is owned by Party A; the (b) "Pinzos" name, logo, local brand, customer relationships and the Company's Local Data and database business records (Section 4) are owned by the Company. Neither Party acquires the other's set; on termination each keeps its own (Section 9.3).
  • 2.7 The Company's Rights Are a Durable Asset. The rights granted to the Company under this Agreement — an exclusive, long-term, escrow-secured right to operate and monetise the Platform across the Territory (the Middle East), together with the buy-out path to full independence (5.6) — constitute a valuable, recognisable intangible asset of the Company. These rights are not revocable at will (2.2, Section 9), survive a good-faith change of control approved under 10.2 (an acquirer stepping into the Company's rights and obligations), and may be recognised on the Company's balance sheet. Party A's ownership of the underlying Platform IP does not diminish the value or durability of the Company's operating rights.

3. Confidentiality

  • 3.1 Each Party keeps the other's non-public information confidential, uses it only to perform this Agreement, and protects it with reasonable care. Survives 5 years after termination; trade secrets for as long as they remain secret.
  • 3.2 The Founders acknowledge the Platform IP is Party A's trade secret and that unauthorised disclosure causes irreparable harm, entitling Party A to injunctive relief.

4. Data

  • 4.1 Local Data. As between the Parties, the Company owns its business data — the contents of the operational database generated by or for the Company's business in the Territory, including property listings and project records, customer, client and lead records, transaction records, and end-user generated and usage data (together, "Local Data") — and is the data controller responsible for UAE PDPL compliance.
  • 4.2 Party A as Processor. Where Party A hosts or accesses Local Data in providing the Platform, it acts as the Company's data processor under the DPA in Schedule 3. Such access does not give Party A ownership of Local Data.
  • 4.3 Anonymized Data Sharing. The Company permits Party A to use, on a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, worldwide basis, fully anonymized, aggregated Platform data via API, solely to improve the Platform; such data must not permit re-identification.
  • 4.4 Data Backups & Access. The Company is entitled to regular automated exports/backups of its Local Data in a standard format (at least monthly and on reasonable request) and to read-only access to its operational data, so the Company always holds its own copy independent of Party A's infrastructure.
  • 4.5 Market Data. Public-source market data for the Territory served within the Platform (e.g., government / Dubai Land Department property-transaction and rental datasets) is available for the Company's business use throughout the Term, and on any exit the Company may retain and continue to use the copies then held in its database (9.3(d)). Party A's proprietary enrichments and data structures (data models, derived analytics, curated geographic boundaries and editorial content) remain part of the Platform IP (2.1).

5. Financial Terms & Equity

5.1 Platform Fee

  • (a) The Company shall pay Party A a continuous Platform Fee equal to 25% of Gross Revenue — Party A's share of platform revenue for contributing, hosting and continuously developing the technology, and its primary economic return. In exchange, Party A permanently waives ordinary dividends (5.3).
  • (b) Where feasible, the Platform Fee is settled automatically at the point of transaction (e.g. Stripe Connect); otherwise paid monthly within 15 days of month-end with a statement.
  • (c) Platform Fee paid on a subsequently refunded transaction is credited against the next payment (or refunded within 30 days).
  • (d) Fixed Rate — Never Diluted. The 25% rate is fixed for the life of this Agreement and is a contractual right of Party A independent of its shareholding. It cannot be reduced, waived, deferred, set off or renegotiated without Party A's written consent, and is unaffected by any change in the Company's cap table, ownership, financing, issuance, reorganisation or structure — even if Party A's equity is diluted to any level or Party A holds no shares. Routing Platform revenue through an affiliate or third party to erode it is a material breach (see 6.3).
  • (e) Fee Tied to Ongoing Service; Abandonment. The Platform Fee is consideration for Party A's continuing provision and maintenance of the Platform under 6.1. If Party A permanently ceases to provide the Platform and its 6.1 services (an "Abandonment") and fails to cure within 30 days of written notice, the Company may invoke the Source Code Escrow (7.3) to self-operate, and the Platform Fee then steps down to 10% of Gross Revenue and thereafter declines in equal annual steps to 0 over 3 years — because, once Party A no longer contributes, the value of the originally-contributed platform depreciates as the Company maintains and develops it itself. The Company may also end the residual fee at any time by a one-time payment of 1× the trailing-12-month residual fee. Party A retains IP ownership throughout. Suspensions permitted under 7.2, temporary outages within the 6.1 standard, and force majeure are not an Abandonment. A bona-fide equity financing may address the Platform Fee via the buy-out in 5.6(c).

5.2 Equity

  • (a) Allocation. On incorporation, Party A (or its designated affiliate) is issued 25% of the fully-diluted share capital, the Founders holding 75%. Party A's shares are ordinary shares, freely dilutable, carrying voting, Reserved-Matter and exit-proceeds rights (25% of any sale/merger/IPO/liquidation proceeds) but no ordinary in-life dividends (5.3). Share certificate delivered within 30 days.
  • (b) Dilution. Party A accepts ordinary pro-rata dilution with no anti-dilution make-whole, and has an optional pre-emption right to participate in new issues. Party A does not rely on its equity percentage — the fixed Platform Fee (5.1) is unaffected by dilution.
  • (c) Board Seat. Party A may appoint one director and receives information rights (monthly accounts, annual audited accounts, cap table).
  • (d) Reserved Matters (Veto). None of the following without Party A's prior written consent: (i) issuing shares to a Founder or a Founder's affiliate at below fair value, or redeeming/reclassifying shares in a way that erodes Party A's rights (bona-fide equity financings to genuine third-party investors do NOT require Party A's consent — Party A accepts dilution per 5.2(b)); (ii) amending the constitutional documents to affect Party A's rights; (iii) related-party transactions or above-market Founder compensation; (iv) amending, assigning or terminating this Agreement or the operating right, or changing the Platform Fee; (v) material debt or security over the Platform or Local Data; (vi) sale of the Company or its main assets; (vii) winding up. Item (iv) is a contractual consent right that survives regardless of Party A's shareholding; the rest operate through Party A's shareholder rights while it holds shares.
  • (e) Locked vs. Flexible. The only permanently locked economic term is the 25% Platform Fee (5.1(d)). Both Parties' equity is freely dilutable, with no vesting or forfeiture. Party A's up-front consideration — a complete platform built at its own cost of approx. US$200,000 — is compensated by IP ownership and the Platform Fee, not share count. Party A's protection against a non-performing partner is the milestone-based exclusivity (2.3) and termination (Section 9).

5.3 Dividends — Permanently Waived

Party A permanently waives ordinary in-life dividends, which accrue 100% to the Founders, in consideration of the Platform Fee (5.1). This is limited to in-life dividends and does not affect Party A's 25% share of any sale/merger/liquidation/IPO proceeds (on exit, pari passu with the Founders) or its voting and Reserved-Matter rights. In short: no dividends, but on exit Party A's stake is treated identically to everyone else's.

5.4 Costs

Third-Party Operating Costs (cloud hosting, storage/CDN, AI/model API) are shared between the Parties pro-rata to their respective equity holdings — currently 25% Party A / 75% the Company — and this split adjusts automatically as equity changes (if Party A is diluted, its share falls accordingly). As Party A operates and controls the infrastructure, it procures and reports these costs each month; Party A's share may be netted against its Platform Fee or settled monthly. All other operating costs (marketing, sales, personnel, RERA/regulatory, office) remain 100% the Company's. The Platform Fee itself is still calculated on gross Platform revenue, before costs.

5.5 Books, Audit & Taxes

  • The Company keeps IFRS books. Party A may audit records relevant to Gross Revenue and the Platform Fee once per year at its cost; an underpayment above 5% shifts the audit cost to the Company.
  • Each Party bears its own taxes and cooperates on structuring for UAE corporate tax, any withholding tax and the Canada–UAE position.

5.6 Party A's Exit — Bundled Sale & Buy-Out

Party A's shares and Platform Fee rights together are the "Party A Interest".

  • (a) Bundled Transfer. Party A may transfer the Party A Interest (equity + Platform Fee stream) together to a third party, subject to the Company's Right of First Refusal in (b). A sale to an outside third party requires the Company's consent, not to be unreasonably withheld (as does separating the equity from the Platform Fee), and any buyer assumes Party A's obligations.
  • (b) Right of First Refusal. Before any third-party sale, Party A offers the Party A Interest to the Founders on the same terms; they have 30 days to accept. If they decline, Party A may sell to the third party within 90 days on no better terms, and the buyer assumes Party A's rights and obligations.
  • (c) Buy-Out / Put Option (at fair market value). After 3 years from first commercial launch, Party A may require the Company to purchase the entire Party A Interest at fair market value (and the Parties may agree such a purchase at any time by mutual consent) — the royalty component priced at a fixed 6× the trailing-12-month Platform Fee: 6 × the total Platform Fee payable under 5.1 for the 12 months immediately preceding the buy-out notice (i.e. 6 × 25% of that period's Gross Revenue); plus the 25% equity at its then fair market value — if the Parties cannot agree the equity value, it is fixed by an independent valuer jointly appointed (or one each, averaged). Payable within 90 days or by instalments. On completion, the ongoing Platform Fee ends and the Company receives a perpetual, royalty-free right to operate and maintain the Platform for its own business anywhere in the Territory (the Middle East) (with a deployable code copy released from escrow), so it can keep running uninterrupted. This buy-out is a right to use a copy, not a transfer of ownership: Party A retains ownership of the Platform IP and may use it in its own markets outside the Middle East (e.g. North America), but shall not provide or sell the Platform to a direct competitor of the Company within the Middle East. This full buy-out is the clean way for the Company to become fully independent of the fee.
  • (d) The Company's Code-Copy Option (technology security — partnership continues). After 3 years from first commercial launch, the Company may elect to purchase a perpetual, non-exclusive, non-transferable right to a deployable copy of the then-current Platform, for the Company's own business within the Territory, while the partnership otherwise continues unchanged: Party A retains its equity (5.2) and the Platform Fee continues at 25% of Gross Revenue (5.1), re-characterised, to the extent the Company operates its own copy, as a continuing licence royalty for the Platform IP. Terms: (i) price — a one-time payment equal to the greater of US$200,000 and 0.5× the trailing-12-month Platform Fee; (ii) the copy licence is conditional on continued payment of the Platform Fee and compliance with 6.3, with automatic settlement of Party A's 25% at the point of transaction where technically feasible, and Party A's audit right under 5.5 increasing to twice per year including access to payment-processor reports; (iii) if the Platform Fee is unpaid and not cured within 30 days of written notice, the copy licence is suspended until payment is made (use during an unpaid period is unlicensed), and persistent non-payment is a material breach (Section 9); (iv) while the Company operates its own copy, Party A's obligations under 6.1 and 6.4 are suspended and Section 5.1(e) does not apply (the Company's choice to self-operate is not an Abandonment); the Parties may agree Party A's continued hosting, maintenance or development as an additional service; (v) Section 2.4 continues to apply, and the copy may not be sub-licensed, sold or transferred. All Platform IP remains Party A's. A copy licence already purchased under this 5.6(d) survives any later termination of this Agreement for so long as the Company continues to pay the Platform Fee (which survives as the licence royalty), unless termination was for the Company's uncured material breach — in which case the licence ends with the Agreement; 9.3(a) applies subject to this sentence.

6. Roles & Obligations

  • 6.1 Party A. Provide, host and maintain the Platform; deliver updates and security patching; protect the architecture. Party A shall use commercially reasonable efforts to keep the Platform available, targeting 99.0% monthly uptime, measured monthly and excluding: (i) scheduled maintenance notified 24 hours ahead; (ii) third-party outages (cloud, CDN, DNS, payment/AI providers); (iii) force majeure; (iv) issues caused by the Company or its users. Failure to meet the target is not a material breach and gives no termination or damages right; the Company's sole remedy is a pro-rata service credit against the Platform Fee. No warranty of uninterrupted or error-free operation.
  • 6.2 Party B / Company. Complete UAE incorporation and obtain/maintain brokerage licenses (e.g. RERA); fund and execute all localized marketing, sales, personnel and go-to-market; fund its 75% share of Third-Party Operating Costs (5.4); comply with 6.3; surface the AI disclaimer (8.3); and actively market and grow the Platform to meet the performance milestones in Schedule 2 (exclusivity condition, Section 2.3). No fixed capital contribution is required — the Company funds growth as it sees fit.
  • 6.3 Anti-Bypass. All platform fees and subscriptions must be processed through the authorised digital payment gateway. Collecting off-gateway payments to avoid the Platform Fee is a material breach.
  • 6.4 Continuous Development (included — no extra charge). As an active partner (not a passive technology supplier), Party A includes in the Platform Fee not only bug fixes, security patches and maintenance, but also ongoing product development and new features that improve the Platform — at no additional charge. Party A shall continuously improve the Platform in good faith and owns and prioritises the product roadmap, giving good-faith consideration to the Company's feature requests. Only an extraordinary, bespoke project that is outside the general product roadmap and materially resource-intensive (or carries significant third-party cost) may, by mutual written agreement, be scoped and priced separately — this is the exception, not the norm.

7. Technical Security & Suspension

  • 7.1 Root Access. As operator of the infrastructure, Party A retains administrative/root access and DNS control necessary to run and protect the Platform, exercised as data processor (Schedule 3), not to appropriate Local Data or harm the Company except under 7.2.
  • 7.2 Payment Protection — Temporary Pause (last resort). A narrow, reversible safeguard for the one thing the partnership relies on — that Party A is actually paid. If the Company fails to pay the Platform Fee or breaches the anti-bypass rule (6.3) and does not cure within 10 business days of written notice, Party A may temporarily pause the service until payment is made, whereupon it is immediately restored. A delay caused by payment-processor settlement times, cross-border transfer processing or bank compliance checks, where the Company has already initiated the payment, does not constitute a failure to pay. A pause is a last resort, not a penalty; all other issues are handled through the good-faith process in Section 9. (As Party A operates the infrastructure, this merely confirms the legal right to pause for non-payment — it does not change the partners' cooperative relationship.)
  • 7.3 Source Code Escrow (deferred — the Company's option, not required at the outset). This step is not required early on. At any time after the first anniversary of commercial launch (or earlier by mutual agreement), the Company may, at its cost, establish and fund a source code escrow with a reputable third-party escrow agent (Schedule 5); within 30 days of it being funded, Party A shall deposit and keep current a copy of the Platform source code and deployment materials. The escrow is released to the Company solely on a "Release Event": (a) Party A's bankruptcy, insolvency or dissolution; (b) an uncured Abandonment (5.1(e)); or (c) Party A's uncured material breach that prevents operation. Release procedure: the Company must give the escrow agent and Party A written notice with reasonable evidence of the Release Event; Party A then has 20 business days to cure or to dispute in good faith, and if Party A disputes, the agent shall not release pending resolution under Section 10.3. On a valid release, the Company may use the released code only to operate and maintain the Platform for its own business within the Territory — not to sub-license or sell it — and Party A retains ownership of all Platform IP. The Platform Fee position in 5.1(e) then applies. Escrow fees (a service fee, not a deposit) are borne by the Company.

8. Warranties, Liability & Indemnity

  • 8.1 Warranty. Party A warrants it owns/may provide the Platform and that, to its knowledge, its self-authored core code does not infringe third-party IP (excluding third-party components/APIs/data, provided under their own terms).
  • 8.2 Liability Cap. Except for breaches of confidentiality/IP (Sections 2–3) and each Party's indemnity, neither Party is liable for indirect or consequential loss; each Party's aggregate liability is capped at the Platform Fees paid in the 12 months before the claim. The Platform is provided "as-is" beyond the 6.1 standard.
  • 8.3 AI / No Advice. Platform outputs (ROI projections, area analyses, investment estimates) are informational only and are not financial, investment, legal or valuation advice. The Company surfaces this to end users and is responsible for local presentation/marketing.
  • 8.4 Indemnities. The Company/Founders indemnify Party A for claims arising from local operation, marketing, brokerage conduct, regulatory non-compliance or presentation of outputs. Party A indemnifies the Company for third-party claims that its self-authored core code infringes their IP (subject to 8.2, except up to US$250,000 for Party A's IP indemnity).

9. Term, Termination & Exit

  • 9.1 Term — Continuing Partnership. This Agreement runs from the Effective Date as a continuing, long-term partnership with no fixed expiry and no renewal cliff. Starting on the 3rd anniversary of first commercial launch — aligned with the buy-out and code-copy options in 5.6 — and every 3 years thereafter, either Party may request a good-faith review of the operating terms; any adjustment takes effect only by written agreement of both Parties (10.4), and absent agreement this Agreement simply continues unchanged. This Agreement ends only through its own exit mechanisms: a buy-out under 5.6, or termination for cause under 9.2.
  • 9.2 Termination (last resort). Only as a last resort, after the notice-and-cure process has run its course, either Party may terminate for the other's uncured material default, insolvency, or a prohibited change of control (10.2).
  • 9.3 Effect / Clean Exit. On termination or dissolution: (a) the Company's right to operate ends and it stops using the Platform; (b) the Company keeps the "Pinzos" brand and clients (subject to (d)); (c) Party A keeps all Platform IP and may redeploy globally — Party A's own market focus is North America. To give the Company comfort, Party A shall not, during the Term and for 24 months after termination, operate or provide the Platform to a direct competitor of the Company anywhere in the Territory (the Middle East), and confirms it has no intention of competing with the Company in that region (this regional non-compete does not apply where termination was for the Company's uncured material breach); (d) the Company retains all of its Local Data (its property under 4.1, with backups under 4.4), and Party A takes no Middle East customer data on exit; the Parties run an orderly wind-down of paying customers; (e) Sections 2.1, 2.4, 3, 8, 10 survive.
  • (f) Platform Buy-Out at Exit (optional, Party A's discretion). The Company may elect to acquire a perpetual, non-exclusive right to operate and a deployable frozen copy of the then-current Platform, for use within the Territory (the Middle East), for a payment agreed or per the 5.6(c) formula. Party A may refuse any price it considers inadequate and retains ownership of the underlying IP (a right to use a copy, not an assignment). Absent purchase, 9.3(c) applies.
  • 9.4 Good-Faith Default Handling (no penalties). The Parties will address any default cooperatively and in good faith, as partners. Before exercising any remedy, the affected Party gives written notice and a reasonable cure period (see 5.1(e), 7.2). No default by either Party triggers any penalty, liquidated damages, or any forced or discounted sale of a Party's interest. Available remedies are limited to those expressly stated (suspension, Platform-Fee step-down, escrow release, or termination with an orderly wind-down). In every case Party A retains ownership of the Platform IP, and any buy-out under 5.6 proceeds at fair market value or its agreed formula regardless of fault — never as a penalty.

10. General

  • 10.1 Pre-Incorporation. Until the Company is incorporated and adopts this Agreement, the Founders are personally, jointly and severally bound. During this interim period the Founders may operate and monetise the business in their personal capacity through a designated bank account and the authorised payment gateway, with all terms of this Agreement (including the Platform Fee and 6.3) applying to that interim operation. They shall incorporate the Company and issue Party A's 25% within 6 months (180 days) of the Effective Date; failure entitles Party A to terminate and withdraw the Company's right to operate.
  • 10.2 Assignment / Change of Control. Party A may assign to an affiliate or successor. The Company may not assign this Agreement without Party A's consent. Founders may transfer shares freely, except that Party A's prior written consent (not unreasonably withheld) is required only for a change of control of the Company or a transfer to a direct competitor of Party A; Party A has a right of first refusal on such transfers.
  • 10.3 Governing Law & Disputes. Governed by DIFC law; disputes finally resolved by arbitration under the DIAC Rules, seat DIFC, Dubai, in English, by one arbitrator. Either Party may seek urgent injunctive relief from a competent court.
  • 10.4 Entire agreement; amendments in writing signed by both; severability; notices by email or WhatsApp to the contacts in Schedule 1, deemed received when delivered. Supersedes the prior MOU dated June 30, 2026.
  • 10.5 Counterparts & Electronic Signature. This Agreement may be executed in counterparts and by electronic signature (including via PandaDoc, DocuSign or a comparable e-signature service), each of which is an original and together constitute one instrument. The Parties consent to electronic signing and agree it is valid, binding and enforceable.
